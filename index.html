Squadron Leader Sarnish Kour (Retired) has spent two decades building things that did not exist before — missions, teams, institutions, and ideas. A military officer in the Indian Air Force, a talent strategist at Willis Towers Watson, and the architect of landmark programmes at Thapar Institute of Engineering & Technology, she brings the same quiet discipline to every room she enters.

She is currently a doctoral researcher at LM Thapar School of Management, studying how entrepreneurs develop opportunities for social impact — a question she has been living, not just researching, for years.

What I Believe
☸
Sarbat Da Bhala — for everyone's good
A Punjabi phrase meaning the wellbeing of all. It is a prayer and a principle — the belief that no decision made in isolation is truly sound, and that the measure of any action is what it does for the world beyond yourself. I carry this in every role I have held: in the Air Force, in institution-building, in research on social ventures that exist precisely to serve those the market overlooks.
☸
Integrity is the baseline
Everything else — strategy, influence, relationships — is built on top of this. Without it, nothing else holds. It is not a value I aspire to. It is the one I refuse to negotiate. In uniform, in boardrooms, in research — the standard has stayed the same.
☸
Reverence for all that exists
Not just people. Every stone, every creature, every quiet thing that sustains life deserves respect. A stone that supports a fish in the water, the soil that holds a tree — nothing on this earth is without consequence. This is not sentiment. It is a way of paying attention, and of recognising that we share this earth, not own it.
☸
Know what is yours to carry
Most of what we worry about is not in our control. Most of what we can change, we leave untouched. Clarity about this distinction — what is mine to act on, what is not — is where effective leadership and a calmer life both begin. Ten years in the Air Force reinforced this: when the stakes are real, it is the difference between decisive action and paralysis.
☸
Choose your battles
Not every hill is worth holding. The leaders who endure know which fights matter, when to push, and when to let go — and who can tell the difference in the moment, not only in hindsight. I have been in rooms where everything felt urgent. The ones who moved things forward were rarely the loudest. They were the most discerning.

'Low' or 'Hollow'
Mental Health · Reflection · 2022
"Do you know how it feels to be low?
Not sad. Not broken. Just — low."
Have you ever felt low?
Why would you not have? We are all feeling beings With clowns and frowns With Ups and downs!!
Maybe when your plans are not going straight, Or when you don't get something even after a long wait. Maybe when you have been ditched by dear mates, Or by lovely memories or scenes that tune re-creates. For you, when nobody stands the expected ground, And when you are all alone with no one around. It's perfectly okay if at times you feel low, You are still fortunate to be able to let that feeling go.
However,
Do you know how it is to feel hollow?
When it's unbearable that you have lost somebody so dear, Or the question – 'Why me' is not answered clear.
When you feel lonely in a gathering, When your tears wonder – is someone even bothering.
Earned in years and you have lost those crowns, When you can feel all the time – the never healing wounds.
When you so much want to walk that past again, This time through different choices and different lanes.
Having lost yourself for someone who doesn't even realise, Scary are the moments – when you hear that hollow shrieking noise.
But please do remember – You have come so far and there's nothing you cannot cope, Go to a friend or a listening stranger or a professional help, but don't forget ever – You and the God within you are unbeatable hope.
— Sarnish
